\begin{abstract}
Multimodal decision models aim to support low-latency decisions by directly mapping visual context and runtime-defined choices to probabilities. Building such models requires a scoring interface that accommodates changing candidate sets and produces reliable decision scores. We present Open-VisualJev, a multimodal decision model built on a vision--language backbone with a dynamic candidate-scoring head. The model combines independent candidate encoding with permutation-equivariant set interaction, enabling joint comparison of the available choices while preserving consistency under candidate reordering. Calibration-aware training and held-out temperature scaling refine the resulting probabilities. Experiments on visual relation tasks, fine-grained recognition, and natural visual question answering show that this design preserves decisions under candidate reordering, learns set-dependent preferences, and remains competitive with a complete-candidate language-model readout on standard tasks. Its advantage becomes more pronounced with larger difficult candidate sets. Component analyses further clarify the contributions of candidate interaction, variable-count training, and probability calibration. These findings support explicit candidate-set modeling as a practical design principle for multimodal decision systems.
\end{abstract}
