\section{Introduction}
Vision--language models provide a general-purpose foundation for interpreting visual observations through natural-language instructions. Decision pipelines additionally require low-latency judgments over alternatives specified at runtime. The emerging System One paradigm addresses this requirement through direct probabilistic prediction, with visual decision models extending the interface to image-conditioned tasks~\citep{yu_2026_visualjev,zhou_2026_pixeljev}.

Runtime candidate sets introduce two structural requirements: scores should follow candidate identity under reordering, while relative preferences may depend on the available alternatives. Shared candidate encoding supports the former; joint comparison supplies the latter. Set representation learning offers a principled way to combine these properties~\citep{zaheer_2017_deepsets,lee_2019_set}.

We present OpenVisualJev\footnote{An independently developed research implementation of a Jev-style multimodal decision model.}, a multimodal decision model built on a vision--language backbone with a dynamic candidate-scoring head. The architecture separates visual grounding of individual candidates from comparison within the current set. Tree-masked attention packs shared image and question prefixes with isolated candidate suffixes into one backbone forward. A position-free set module compares the resulting candidate representations, and a shared pointer maps them to a probability distribution over the supplied choices. Calibration-aware training and held-out temperature scaling complete the pipeline (Figure~\ref{fig:set-pointer-method}).

We evaluate order stability and set-dependent preferences through controlled tests, then assess standard recognition and VQA capability on the same Qwen3-VL backbone. The results connect candidate-set structure to stable decisions, stronger recognition with larger difficult candidate sets, and calibrated probability outputs.

Our contributions are threefold:
\begin{itemize}
\item We develop a multimodal decision model that combines pretrained visual--language representations with dynamic candidate scoring and an explicit probability-calibration pipeline.
\item We formulate a candidate-scoring architecture that combines permutation-equivariant set interaction with shared pointer readout, and analyze its order stability and capacity for set-dependent preferences.
\item We evaluate these properties through controlled structural tests, unified fine-grained recognition, and natural VQA, with ablations that characterize candidate interaction, candidate-count training, and probability calibration.
\end{itemize}
